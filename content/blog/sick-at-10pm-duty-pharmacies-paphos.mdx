## Why Google Maps can mislead you after hours

In Cyprus, **Google Maps opening hours for pharmacies are often wrong**—I have shown up to a green *Open* pin more times than I care to admit and found shutters down. After normal hours or on half-days, **do not let the map be your only source**.

What actually runs the island at night is the **duty pharmacy system**: while most pharmacies close by **6:30 PM** (or earlier on **Wednesdays** and **Saturdays**), a smaller set in each district stays open on a **roster**—often until **10 PM or 11 PM** in winter, and **midnight** in summer, depending on the schedule.

## The Wednesday and Saturday half-day rule

On **Wednesdays** and **Saturdays**, most pharmacies close around **1:30 PM**. On those days, the duty pharmacy often **starts earlier** in the afternoon, so the “open late” logic you use on a Monday may not apply.

## Before you drive: practical checks

- **Always call first:** Before you drive across Paphos, call the number on the duty roster or the pharmacy’s own line. Even correct-looking tools can miss a short break or a last-minute change—and Maps rarely reflects that in real time.
- **Keep your “Yellow Slip” or ID handy:** If you are buying prescription medication under **GESY**, you will need your identification.
- **Expect a small premium:** Some items may include a modest night-service fee, though this is less common now.

## When a late-night problem is not “just a pharmacy trip”

Duty pharmacies are essential for many after-hours situations—but **chest pain, trouble breathing, sudden neurological symptoms, severe bleeding, or a seriously unwell child** are not solved by a detour to a chemist.

Treat **duty pharmacy lists** the same way you would treat any third-party roster: **use two sources**, **call to confirm**, and **assume nothing** from a map pin alone.
